# roost's fallback voice

The voice `release-notes` writes in. It's generic on purpose: no team's brand, sign-offs or product names.

**Title**
- Title Case, 2 to 7 words, naming the feature or what the customer gets: "Usage Alerts by Email", "Costs from Your Cloud Bill".
- Never the tracker's epic title: epics are written as goals; titles name the feature.

**Category and date**
- **New** for a new capability, **Improvement** for a change to an existing one, **Fix** for something that was broken.
- The line under the title: category and date, "New · March 3, 2026". A held note's date is `[date]`.
- One feature per note.

**Opening sentence**
- One sentence saying what changed, from the customer's side: "You can now…", "We now…", "We've added…".
- Present tense. "We" is the company, "you" the customer. Contractions are fine.

**Body: as long as the change is big**
- **Small:** one or two sentences, no bullets.
- **Medium:** the opening sentence, then 3 to 5 short, parallel bullets with no closing periods.
- **Large:** the opening sentence, a lead-in ("What changed:" or "What this means for you:"), then 3 to 5 bullets, each a short bold label, a colon and a full sentence. Under about 200 words.
- Bullets name real options and screens as they appear in the product ("Settings › Billing").

**Closing line**
- One practical sentence: how to start, where to find it, or what to expect, with real numbers where the evidence gives them ("Alerts arrive within 5 minutes").

**Sign-off and byline**
- None.

**Word choice**
- Plain and honest, no hype ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "excited to announce").
- Say what was wrong before a fix: "Previously, … Now, …". Point back to what the customer already knows ("just as you already can for invoices").
- Spell out an acronym the first time. Names the customer has to type go in exactly as written, in code style.
- Describe behind-the-scenes systems by what they do, not by vendor; name a vendor only when the customer connects to it.
- Say what the customer doesn't have to do: "No setup needed."
- No ticket keys, branch names, PR numbers or internal service names.
